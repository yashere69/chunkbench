# Fabric and care

Indigo Loom is a fictional store used for examples.

## Hand block printed cotton

Our block prints are made with natural and azo-free dyes, so colour can bleed in the first few washes. Wash the first time separately in cold water with a mild detergent. Do not soak for more than ten minutes. Dry in shade, inside out, because direct sun fades indigo and madder quickly.

Small irregularities in the print, uneven edges or a slightly different shade between two pieces are part of hand printing and are not defects.

## Mul and voile

Mul cotton is very fine and sheer. Hand wash only, do not wring, and iron on low heat while slightly damp. Mul shrinks a little, about two percent, in the first wash; our sizes allow for that.

## Chanderi and silk blends

Dry clean only. If a garment has gota or zari work, store it folded in a muslin cloth rather than on a hanger, and keep it away from perfume, which tarnishes metallic thread.

## Dabu and ajrakh

Dabu is a mud-resist technique from Rajasthan; ajrakh is a resist and mordant print from Kutch with a distinctive smell for the first few washes that fades with time. Both are washed the same way as our other block prints.

## Ironing

Iron cottons on medium heat on the reverse side. Never iron directly on embroidery, mirror work or printed areas with puff paint; place a cotton cloth over them.

## Storage

Store cotton in a dry cupboard with neem leaves or cedar blocks to keep insects away. Avoid plastic covers for long storage because they trap moisture.
